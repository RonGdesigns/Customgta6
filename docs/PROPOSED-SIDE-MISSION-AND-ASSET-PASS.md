# Bloodlines — Proposed Side-Mission Revisions & Stock-Asset Feasibility Pass

**Status:** Superseded — the approved items were implemented on September 9, 2026. See `HANDOFF-IMPLEMENTATION-UPDATE.md` for what was built and `CHANGE-REGISTER.md` for the per-item decisions. Kept for the reasoning behind each choice; do not re-apply from here.  
**Companion to:** `docs/PROPOSED-STORY-CHANGES.md` and `docs/PROPOSED-STORY-CHANGES-BATCH3-4.md`

This document records proposed side-mission character revisions, possible additional side missions for character development, and a stock-GTA-asset feasibility pass. It does **not** change mission scripts, dialogue data, scenes, coordinates, or runtime behavior.

# 1. Side-Mission Character Pass

## SM01 — Lead & Kevlar

**Assessment:** Protect.

Ice going alone to correct the ammunition deal is appropriate. Ron's role is simply making sure Ice checks in. Protect Ice's aftermath admission that he almost switched the radio off out of habit. Protect Gohan's simple response that he heard him and they will check the ammunition together when he returns.

Do not add more emotional explanation.

## SM02 — Zero-Day Injection

**Assessment:** Strong Gohan mission; HUD cleanup only.

Protect Ice's line that Gohan is not asking permission by giving a check-in time; he is giving his brothers a chance to notice if something goes wrong.

Move spoken tutorial language such as "reach the roof marker" and "two marked guards" to HUD/objective text.

Protect Ron's aftermath:

> "And you're back on the radio. I like that part of the upgrade best."

This is a clean way to show Gohan improving at communication.

## SM03 — Midnight Drift

**Assessment:** Strong Ron mission; light cleanup.

Protect Ron's relationship with KJ and the line establishing that Ron wants his friend around after the finish.

Move "three laps through the yellow checkpoints" to HUD.

Use "Guess never fails a delivery" sparingly. Ron's clutch reputation should come mainly from gameplay rather than repeated self-description.

## SM04 — Dead Drop Quarry

**Assessment:** Protect with minor tone polish.

The mission is a strong Ice showcase and works well as a solo sniper mission.

Suggested Ron aftermath tone:

> **RON:** "Good. Send that shit to both of us."

The important beat is that Ice shares the recovered frequencies instead of keeping the only copy himself.

## SM05 — Black Box Estuary

**Assessment:** Strong Gohan mission; adjust aftermath framing.

Protect Gohan calling when he finds the buoy, calling when the signal is locked, and saying he is coming back. These small check-ins are the character development.

Replace the old "stop being useful long enough to eat" framing with a more familiar brotherly line.

Suggested direction:

> **ICE:** "Job's done. Come eat before Ron starts calling your phone again."

This reinforces Gohan's real habit without implying he has to earn his place in the trio.

## SM06 — Canyon Runner

**Assessment:** Strong Ron development mission; rewrite aftermath.

The important clutch is Ron realizing that slowing down is the right move instead of forcing a dangerous blind corner.

Replace the overly reflective aftermath with something more natural:

> **GOHAN:** "You actually slowed down."
>
> **RON:** "Don't sound so surprised."
>
> **GOHAN:** "I am."

This preserves the character beat without sounding like therapy dialogue.

## SM07 — Blood Debt

**Assessment:** Strong premise; substantial aftermath rewrite.

Protect Ice being honest that Sterling is personal and that he is not hiding the decision behind the larger campaign.

Suggested Ron intro response:

> **RON:** "That's your call. Just come back when it's done."

Replace the explanatory aftermath with something much quieter:

> **ICE:** "Sterling's dead."
>
> **RON:** "You good?"
>
> **ICE:** "Nah."
>
> **RON:** "Aight. Come back."

The scene should not explain Ice's entire emotional state. The brotherhood is established enough for short lines to carry the weight.

## SM08 — Burner Protocol

**Assessment:** Strong Gohan premise; major character reframing.

Gohan's problem is not whether Ron and Ice accept him or whether he deserves to belong. They already see him as their brother.

The mission should instead reinforce that Gohan tends to disappear into his own problems and handle them alone.

Suggested Ice intro direction:

> **ICE:** "Take whatever proves what they did. And don't disappear for two days after."

Suggested aftermath:

> **GOHAN:** "Files are out. Archive is gone."
>
> **RON:** "Cool. You coming back?"
>
> **GOHAN:** "Yeah."
>
> **RON:** "That's all I needed."

## SM09 — The Long Exit

**Assessment:** Strong Ron mission; protect.

This mission fits Ron's role especially well because he is not just securing his own escape. He verifies the passenger count and makes sure the whole crew can use the route.

Protect KJ warning Ron to keep a backup exit. That reinforces Ron's established habit of always planning another way out.

# 2. Additional Side-Mission Concepts for Character Development

These are optional proposals. They are designed to use existing GTA V locations, vehicles, peds, weapons, and scripted camera work only.

## Proposed SM10 — MISSED CALLS

**Focus:** Gohan's disappearing habit; Ron/Ice shorthand; awkward Gohan clutch.

**Placement:** Mid-Act II, after the trio has re-established trust but before Gohan's communication arc is fully resolved.

**Premise:** Gohan goes quiet while checking a remote relay/scrapyard lead. Ron calls repeatedly and gets no answer. Ron and Ice drive out to find him, mostly annoyed rather than panicked.

**Gameplay:**

- Ron drives to the location with Ice.
- Ice clears a small hostile perimeter.
- They find Gohan halfway through a strange technical setup he never told them about.
- Hostiles arrive because Gohan's experiment accidentally exposed the site.
- Gohan triggers an improvised jammer/industrial-machine trap that sounds ridiculous when he explains it but wipes out the enemy vehicles.

**Character payoff:**

Ron gets to say some version of:

> "You got every satellite in San Andreas and still can't answer a damn phone."

Gohan's clutch works, but Ron still makes him promise to call next time.

**Asset feasibility:** Green. Use a stock scrapyard, relay yard, industrial lot, stock peds/vehicles, scripted explosions and existing props.

## Proposed SM11 — BACKUP PLAN

**Focus:** Ron and Ice's dependable bond; Ice clutch.

**Placement:** Late Act II or early Act III.

**Premise:** Ron is running a vehicle pickup alone when the deal turns into an ambush. He calls Ice once. Ice does not ask for a long explanation; he simply shows up.

**Gameplay:**

- Ron survives a short pursuit while trying to keep the objective vehicle alive.
- Player switches to Ice on a rooftop or ridge.
- Ice removes the lead pursuers/snipers and opens Ron's escape lane.
- Return to Ron for the extraction.

**Character payoff:**

Minimal dialogue:

> **RON:** "Good looking."
>
> **ICE:** "Always."

This reinforces why Ron naturally leans on Ice without turning either man into the other's subordinate.

**Asset feasibility:** Green. Existing road network, rooftops, vehicles, sniper mechanics.

## Proposed SM12 — OLD ROUTE

**Focus:** Trio history without a giant firefight.

**Placement:** After M11 or before the campaign leaves Los Santos for Blaine County.

**Premise:** Ron needs to move a low-value vehicle/parts load across south Los Santos. Ice and Gohan ride with him. The route passes several places from their youth.

**Gameplay:**

- Mostly driving and conversation.
- One small interruption: police checkpoint, broken-down truck, or low-level gang obstruction.
- Ron handles the route; Ice/Gohan banter about old memories.

**Character payoff:**

This creates room for the football corner, car-wash stories, missed calls, and other history without forcing every memory into a high-stakes mission.

**Asset feasibility:** Green. Entirely stock city streets, vehicles, peds and cameras.

## Proposed SM13 — THREE SEATS

**Focus:** Brotherhood/downtime; Ron as glue.

**Placement:** Late campaign, before the final endgame run, or as an optional post-M60 calm mission.

**Premise:** No major score. Ron insists all three go get food before the next operation. Gohan is late/not answering. Ice and Ron go pick him up.

**Gameplay:**

- Short drive.
- Find Gohan at a work site/studio/parking structure.
- Minor interruption can be optional: somebody tails them, a stolen vehicle blocks the route, or a low-level chase develops.
- Mission ends with the trio sitting at a diner/parking lot/food stand rather than with a huge explosion.

**Character payoff:**

This gives the campaign one genuinely low-stakes brotherhood mission and sets up the final "breakfast / three seats" motif without waiting until the final scene.

**Asset feasibility:** Green if staged at an exterior diner/food stand/parking lot. No custom interior required.

# 3. Stock GTA V Asset Feasibility — Current Reality

The current repository already contains a technical feasibility pass that classifies the campaign as **46 Green, 26 Yellow and 7 Red**. Green means stock systems can do it directly; Yellow means a substitute/stand-in/interior or faked process may be needed; Red means the event should be faked with camera cuts, attachments, teleports, prop swaps and scripted paths rather than physically simulated.

A strict **"no downloaded/new assets"** rule is possible for most of the campaign, but not always with the exact location/interior imagery originally imagined.

The important distinction is:

- **Gameplay concept can usually survive.**
- **Exact custom environment may need to become a stock GTA location/interior substitute.**

Examples:

- A custom sea cave becomes the existing Paleto cave or another stock coastal space.
- A custom maintenance vault becomes an existing warehouse/tunnel/service interior.
- A custom penthouse becomes a stock accessible high-rise/interior shell or an exterior rooftop command post.
- A bespoke offshore rig can be represented by a stock offshore/industrial platform, dock structure, large vessel/deck, or carefully staged prop/structure substitute.
- A collapsing structure is never physically simulated; use camera shake, fades, explosions, swaps and a player jump.

No-new-asset implementation therefore requires a **stock-substitution pass** for every Yellow mission before scripting the final environment.

# 4. Opening Prologue Feasibility With No New Assets

The proposed opening — Ron arrives at LSIA, the player takes control, drives to Ron's apartment/home, then receives the dockyard job — is one of the easiest sequences in the campaign to build with only installed GTA assets.

## Recommended stock-only implementation

1. **Opening establishing shot at LSIA**
   - Use scripted in-engine cameras over the terminal/exterior roadway.
   - Spawn Ron using the existing Guess ped/model and appearance system.
   - Use stock luggage/airport pedestrians/taxis/traffic if desired.

2. **Player handoff**
   - Fade from the opening camera to Ron standing at the curb or beside a vehicle.
   - Restore player control.
   - Objective: drive home.

3. **Drive to Ron's home**
   - Use ordinary GTA road traffic and GPS route.
   - Character dialogue can play over radio/phone or as Ron's own lines.

4. **Home arrival**
   - The current repo already treats the starter homes as exterior access points rather than furnished interiors. That means the prologue can work immediately without a custom apartment interior.
   - Ron arrives at the Burro Heights home/chop-shop marker.
   - Short exterior/street/garage-door scene triggers.
   - He receives the M01 job.

5. **Transition to M01**
   - Either return player control and have Ron drive to Terminal Island, or fade/time-cut to the mission-start area if pacing demands it.

## If an interior is desired later

A stock interior can be substituted, but it is not required for the prologue to work. A custom furnished Ron apartment would be a separate asset decision, not a prerequisite for the story opening.

# 5. Cutscene Feasibility

The repo already has an in-engine `CutsceneDirector` that:

- creates scripted cameras;
- freezes/holds actors and vehicles during scenes;
- frames nearby characters in person;
- uses phone/radio framing for separated characters;
- spawns temporary stock peds, vehicles and props;
- restores camera/control afterward;
- supports skippable scenes;
- can perform scene actions at the end of dialogue.

That means the campaign does **not** require pre-rendered video cutscenes.

Most scenes can be built as Rockstar-style in-engine sequences using:

- camera positions and cuts;
- ped task/scenario animations;
- enter/exit vehicle tasks;
- phone/radio dialogue framing;
- fades to hide teleports or scene resets;
- stock props and vehicles;
- particle effects/explosions;
- subtitles/voice audio.

The limitation is bespoke animation. If a script says a character performs a very specific motion GTA does not have, either select the closest stock animation/scenario or rewrite/block the shot so the camera implies the action.

# 6. Mission Start / Transition / End Layout

The current structural model is fundamentally correct for GTA-style missions:

1. **Trigger / availability** — mission becomes available after prerequisites.
2. **Start point** — player enters a marker, accepts/starts, or receives a scripted handoff.
3. **Intro scene / briefing** — short in-engine scene or radio briefing.
4. **Gameplay setup** — mission-specific peds, vehicles, weapons, blips and objectives are spawned/configured.
5. **Stage progression** — objective completion advances through discrete mission stages.
6. **Mid-mission scenes** — recognition/reveal/set-piece cut can pause or bridge stages where necessary.
7. **Checkpoints / failure rules** — death, arrest, destroyed key vehicle, wrong target killed, etc. fail/recover cleanly.
8. **Final objective / extraction** — mission does not end merely because the main target is complete; the crew reaches the intended safe/extraction state.
9. **Outro / aftermath** — short reflective scene or radio exchange.
10. **Cleanup / progression** — temporary entities, cameras, objective state and control are released; completion is saved and the next mission becomes eligible.

That is the right architecture.

The main layout rule to enforce during implementation is: **the outro should never fire before the gameplay state is genuinely safe and complete.** For example, if all three must leave in one vehicle, mission completion should wait until the actual living crew are inside the correct getaway and the final escape condition is met.

The repo's M01 work already follows this philosophy by preserving actual actor positions/seats through recognition and requiring real extraction behavior rather than teleporting the crew together for a scene.

# 7. Stock-Only Risks That Still Require Live GTA Testing

Repository tests and static validation are useful but do not prove every scene works in the real engine. The project's QA documentation explicitly notes that stand-in tests do not prove live terrain, native stability or rendered animations.

Before promising a strict no-new-asset campaign, live-test at least:

- exact airport spawn/camera positions;
- Ron's home exterior and approach path;
- every intended stock interior substitute;
- offshore-rig/platform stand-in geometry;
- aircraft/vehicle model availability in the installed GTA build;
- camera clipping and actor animation at scene locations;
- mission cleanup after skipped cutscenes;
- checkpoint restore around vehicles/attached props;
- DLC/Online vehicle models used in Story Mode;
- scene transitions that teleport actors/entities during fades.

---

# 8. Recommendation

Keep the **no new downloadable assets** goal as the default.

Build the prologue entirely from stock LSIA + Ron's existing exterior home first. Do not make a custom apartment interior a blocker.

Then perform a mission-by-mission **stock substitution lock** for the Yellow missions before implementation. Only if a stock substitute genuinely hurts the story or readability should a custom asset become optional later.

For side missions, add SM10–SM13 only if pacing allows them. They are intentionally designed to deepen the trio without requiring new models, interiors or map assets.
